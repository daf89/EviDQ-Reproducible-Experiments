Box 4: Advantages and disadvantages of ACE inhibitors plus CCBs 
Advantages:
••
Single-pill combination medication increases ease of logistics and reduces patient 
barriers;
••
Reduces the number of steps in the protocol, increasing the likelihood of control of 
blood pressure;
••
Reduces number of pills and increases adherence as most patients will require 
multiple anti-hypertensive medications;
••
Benefits some patients with kidney disease, prior myocardial infarction, and low 
ejection fraction;
••
Reduced incidence of ankle edema compared to CCB use without ACE-inhibitors or 
ARBs.
Disadvantages:
••
Single-pill combination therapy may be more expensive than other hypertension 
medications given as single agent drugs;
••
Not all patients would have required both medications;
••
A persistent cough is experienced by up to 10% of patients treated with an ACE 
inhibitor; risk is higher in people of African descent;
••
Small risk of angioedema; risk is higher in people of African descent;
••
ACE inhibitors (and ARBs) should NOT be given to women who are or who may 
become pregnant;
••
Risk of hyperkalemia, especially if patient has CKD.
Note: Research suggests that ARBs may have effectiveness similar to ACE inhibitors.
