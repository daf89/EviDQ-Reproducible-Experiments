8. 	 Availability of trained health care staff for diabetes management
Indicator name
Availability of trained health care staff for diabetes management
Purpose
To assess the availability of high-quality services for diabetes management.
Definition
Proportion of health facilities in which staff have been trained in the latest WHO 
or national guidelines on diabetes clinical management.
Numerator
Number of health facilities in which staff have been trained in the latest 
WHO or national guidelines on diabetes clinical management during the 
reporting period.
“Staff” refers to physicians or other nationally authorized health professionals for 
diabetes management. A multidisciplinary care team for diabetes management 
may include the following:
	
●
cardiologist 
	
●
diabetes nurse
	
●
endocrinologist
	
●
general practitioner 
	
●
homecare nurse
	
●
nephrologist
	
●
nutritionist
	
●
ophthalmologist
	
●
physiotherapist
	
●
podiatrist
Denominator
Total number of health facilities
Method of estimation
Numerator ÷ denominator × 100
Disaggregation
Facility level of care type, provider ownership type (public/private), facility 
location type (urban/rural), training topic, type of health professional
Sources of data
Health facility report, health workforce information system or survey
Limitations/comments
Frequency of training depends on the date of the last version of WHO or national 
guidelines, as well as the staff turnover at the health care facility.

Guidance on global monitoring for diabetes prevention and control: framework, indicators and application
9. 	 Availability of diabetes, cardiovascular disease and hypertension 
core medicines
Indicator name
Availability of diabetes, cardiovascular disease and hypertension 
core medicines
Purpose
To monitor supply of essential medicines for diabetes management.
Definition
Proportion of health facilities that have diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and 
hypertension core medicines based on WHO or national treatment guidelines.
Diabetes core medicines include:
	
●
metformin
	
●
sulfonylurea
	
●
insulin
Cardiovascular disease core medicines include:
	
●
aspirin
	
●
beta blockers
	
●
statins
Hypertension core medicines include the following classes of medications:
	
●
angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors
	
●
angiotensin-receptor blockers
	
●
long-acting dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers
	
●
thiazide and thiazide-like agents
Other core medicines may be added to the list as WHO and national guidelines 
are updated. 
Numerator
Number of health facilities reporting “no stock-out” of diabetes, cardiovascular 
disease and hypertension core medicines during the reporting period.
Denominator
Total number of health facilities
Method of estimation
Numerator ÷ denominator × 100
Disaggregation
Type of medicine, type of insulin (short-acting human, intermediate acting 
human, long-acting analogue), facility level of care type, provider ownership 
type (public/private), facility location type (urban/rural).
Sources of data
Health facility medicine stock register, health facility report, logistics information 
system or survey
Limitations/comments
In some settings, health facilities do not dispense medicines so the reporting 
units may be community medicine dispensaries/pharmacies.
The preferred data source among the sources listed for this indicator depends on 
the data source quality in the local context.
