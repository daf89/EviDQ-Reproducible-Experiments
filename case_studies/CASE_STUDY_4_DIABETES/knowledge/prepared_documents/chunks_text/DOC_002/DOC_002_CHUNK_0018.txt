6. 	 Inclusion of insulin, insulin delivery devices and blood glucose self-
monitoring in national health benefit package
Indicator name
Inclusion of insulin, insulin delivery devices and blood glucose self-
monitoring in national health benefit package 
Purpose
To assess integration of insulin, insulin delivery devices and blood glucose self-
monitoring into national health benefit packages.
Definition
Country has a national health benefit package that includes insulin, insulin 
delivery devices and blood glucose self-monitoring.
Health benefit packages define the high priority services in a country that should 
be made available to all citizens from public funds.
Numerator
Not applicable
Denominator
Not applicable
Method of estimation
Country can respond “Yes” to the question “Does the country have a national 
health benefit package that includes insulin, insulin delivery devices and blood 
glucose self-monitoring?” and needs to provide a copy of the national health 
benefit package.
Inclusion of insulin and insulin delivery devices in the national health benefit 
package is validated by examining the national health benefit package document 
for insulin, insulin delivery devices and blood glucose self-monitoring coverage.
The indicator is considered “Yes” if the country has a national health benefit 
package that includes insulin, insulin delivery devices and blood glucose 
self-monitoring.
The indicator is considered “Partially yes” if the country has a national health 
benefit package that includes either insulin or insulin delivery devices or blood 
glucose self-monitoring but not all.
The indicator is considered “No” if the country has a national health benefit 
package, but it does not include insulin, insulin delivery devices or blood glucose 
self-monitoring.
Disaggregation
Component of the indicator (insulin or insulin delivery devices or blood glucose 
self-monitoring), type of diabetes, type of insulin, level of reimbursement (fully 
reimbursed or partially reimbursed), age group of people who are included in the 
benefit package, administrative level (national provincial/state/region or district).
Sources of data
NCD country capacity survey, other survey, country report (national health 
benefit package list) 
Limitations/comments
None
Related links

Guidance on global monitoring for diabetes prevention and control: framework, indicators and application
7. 	 Insulin, insulin delivery devices and blood glucose self-monitoring 
affordability 
Indicator name
Insulin, its delivery devices, and blood glucose self-monitoring affordability
Purpose
To assess the affordability of insulin, insulin delivery devices and blood glucose 
self-monitoring.
Definition
Country has affordable insulin, insulin delivery devices and blood glucose self-
monitoring for people with diabetes. Affordability means that the monthly 
expenses for people living with diabetes or their families, including expenditures 
on basic needs represented by national poverty line (NPL), do not exceed the 
minimum wage for the lowest paid government sector worker (LPGW).
Numerator
Not applicable
Denominator
Not applicable
Method of estimation
The indicator is considered “Yes”, that is, affordable, if:
[(NPL +monthly cost for insulin, insulin devices and blood glucose self-
monitoring to person with diabetes) /LPGW wage] ≤1
Disaggregation
Type of diabetes, type of insulin, type of insulin delivery device, type of blood 
glucose self-monitoring.
Sources of data
Country report, survey, national health insurance claims database
Limitations/comments
A population-weighted average can be used to address differences in prices, 
wages and size of population at sub-national levels.
When estimated expenses and wages are derived from different years, general 
inflation rate and health care inflation rate must be included in the calculation.
Related links
Keeping the 100-year-old promise: making insulin access universal. 
Geneva: World Health Organization; 2021 (https://apps.who.int/iris/
handle/10665/348384).
SDG indicator metadata. New York: United Nations; 2019 (https://unstats.un.org/
sdgs/metadata/files/Metadata-03-0B-03.pdf, accessed 26 May 2024).
