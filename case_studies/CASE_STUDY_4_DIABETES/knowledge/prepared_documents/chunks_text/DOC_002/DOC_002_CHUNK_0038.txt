39. 	Blindness among people with diabetes
Indicator name
Blindness among people with diabetes
Purpose
To assess outcome of diabetes management.
Definition
Proportion of people with diabetes who experienced blindness based on WHO or 
national guidelines.
Numerator
Number of people with diabetes registered in the facility who experienced 
blindness during the reporting period.
Blindness is defined by WHO as having a visual acuity in the better eye of 3/60 (at 
3 metres from the vision chart, a person can read a letter that someone with a 
normal vision would be able to see at 60 metres).
Denominator
Total number of people with diabetes registered in the facility.
Method of estimation
Numerator ÷ denominator × 100
Disaggregation
Where possible and applicable, stratify by facility level of care type, provider 
ownership type (public/private), and individual-level characteristics such as age, 
sex, race/ethnicity, comorbidity status, high-risk groups, socioeconomic status, 
residence type (urban/rural), and health insurance type.
Sources of data
Health facility patient registers, health records
Limitations/comments
For comparison with other health care facilities the indicator needs to be 
age-standardized.

40. 	End-stage kidney disease among people with diabetes
Indicator name
End-stage kidney disease among people with diabetes
Purpose
To assess outcome of diabetes management.
Definition
Proportion of people with diabetes who were newly diagnosed with end-stage 
kidney disease based on WHO or national guidelines. 
End-stage kidney disease is defined as a glomerular filtration rate (GFR) of less 
than 15 mL/min/1.73m2.
Numerator
Number of people with diabetes registered in the facility who were newly 
diagnosed with end-stage kidney disease during the reporting period.
Denominator
Total number of people with diabetes registered in the facility.
Method of estimation
Numerator ÷ denominator × 100
Disaggregation
Where possible and applicable, stratify by facility level of care type, provider 
ownership type (public/private), and individual-level characteristics such as age, 
sex, race/ethnicity, comorbidity status, high-risk groups, socioeconomic status, 
residence type (urban/rural), and health insurance type.
Sources of data
Health facility patient registers, health records
Limitations/comments
For comparison with other health care facilities the indicator needs to be 
age-standardized.
