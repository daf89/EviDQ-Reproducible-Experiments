32. 	Hypertension prevalence
Indicator name
Hypertension prevalence
Purpose
To measure the prevalence of hypertension among people aged 18 
years and over.
Definition
Proportion of people aged 18 years and over with raised blood pressure or on 
medication for hypertension.
Raised blood pressure criteria:
	
●
systolic blood pressure (SBP) ≥140 mmHg; or 
	
●
diastolic blood pressure (DBP) ≥90 mmHg. 
Numerator
Number of respondents aged 18 years and over with raised blood pressure or on 
medication for hypertension. 
Denominator
Total number of respondents aged 18 years and over.
Method of calculation
Numerator ÷ denominator × 100
Disaggregation
Where possible and applicable, stratify by individual characteristics such as age, 
sex, race/ethnicity, comorbidity status, high-risk groups, socioeconomic status, 
residence type (urban/rural), and health insurance type.
Sources of data
Nationally or sub-nationally representative population-based surveys with blood 
pressure measurement.
Limitations/comments
For any comparison over time or with other populations, age 
standardization is needed.
Related links
WHO Noncommunicable disease surveillance, monitoring and reporting: 
STEPwise approach to NCD risk factor surveillance (STEPS) [website]. 
Geneva: World Health Organization; 2024 (https://www.who.int/teams/
noncommunicable-diseases/surveillance/systems-tools/steps, accessed 
26 May 2024).

54
Guidance on global monitoring for diabetes prevention and control: framework, indicators and application
33. 	Glycaemic control among people with diabetes based on glycated 
haemoglobin (HbA1c)
Indicator name
Glycaemic control among people with diabetes based on glycated 
haemoglobin (HbA1c)
Purpose
To measure the effectiveness of clinical services for people with diabetes for 
achieving glycaemic control.
Definition
Proportion of people living with diabetes with good glycaemic control based on 
HbA1c measurement.
Based on WHO guidelines:
Clinical target: <7% (53mmol/mol) 
Global target: <8% (64mmol/mol)
	
●
Glycaemic control can be alternatively defined in accordance with 
national targets and guidelines.
	
●
This indicator can be derived from population-level surveys or health 
facility-based routine data. 
Numerator
Population survey data: Number of respondents with diabetes with good 
glycaemic control using HbA1c measurement.
Facility data: Number of people with diabetes registered in health facilities with 
good glycaemic control using HbA1c measurement at the last clinical visit during 
the reporting period, excluding those who were newly diagnosed with less than 
three months of treatment.
Denominator
Population survey data: Total number of respondents with diabetes. 
Facility data: Total number of people with diabetes registered in health facilities 
during the reporting period, excluding those who were newly diagnosed with less 
than three months of treatment.
Method of estimation
Numerator ÷ denominator × 100
Disaggregation
Where possible and applicable, stratify by type of diabetes, facility level of 
care type, provider ownership type (public/private), and individual-level 
characteristics such as age, sex, race/ethnicity, comorbidity status, high-
risk groups, socioeconomic status, residence type (urban/rural), and health 
insurance type.
Sources of data
Nationally or sub-nationally representative population-based surveys, with 
HbA1c measurement, health facility patient registers, health records.
Limitations/comments
People with unknown status of glycaemic control (e.g. missed appointment/
dropped out) and patients referred to higher-level care facilities during the 
reporting quarter will be counted in the denominator and their glycaemic control 
status will be counted as not controlled.
Patients known to have transferred to another facility during the reporting 
quarter will be counted in the denominator and their last known status prior to 
transfer will be used.
For comparison with other health care facilities the indicator needs to be 
age-standardized.
